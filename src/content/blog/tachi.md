The tachi was one of the earliest swords carried by the Japanese samurai. Featuring a longer and more pronounced curve than the later katana, it was designed primarily for use by mounted warriors.

Typically worn suspended from the belt with the cutting edge facing downward, the tachi excelled at delivering sweeping strikes from horseback. It played a central role in Japanese warfare for several centuries.

As combat styles evolved, the tachi gradually gave way to the katana, though it remains an important part of Japan's military and cultural history.
